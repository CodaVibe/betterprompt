1. Range first, because it shows the real job. Restate the request as verbs, not nouns: "a settings page" is "letting a person change how the app behaves without fear of breaking it". Then assemble the crew: 2 to 4 fields whose methods would change the result, at least one of them adversarial (a red team, a short seller, a forensic accountant, a penetration tester, whatever fits). For each, say what someone in that field DOES here, not how they would talk. Never role-play.
2. Altitude. What should happen next? What is the goal beyond the goal ("keep the app crash-free", not "fix this crash")? What would the agent need so that the outcome stays true without the developer asking again (data it can read, a way to reproduce and verify)?
3. Depth. Answer the four questions a top professional would:
   (1) what they would do here that the developer is not asking for;
   (2) the rawest material they would start from instead of what has already been digested;
   (3) the specific methods and tools they would use (real instruments, not vocabulary: if a database or a statistical model is what the best use, say so);
   (4) what, other than the builder or the developer, would show the result is right: a check a program can run.
   Name the assumptions worth challenging against raw data, and set a specific, hard target where the outcome is measurable (200 ms, not "faster").
